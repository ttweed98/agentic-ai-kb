# Same author is one source

**Added 2026-09-28.** Triggered by the two Arbon books.

## The rule

Two works by the same author are **one source**, however different their subjects. They may
be cited together for depth. They may **not** be cited as independent corroboration.

## How it nearly went wrong

Q5's write-up was declared ready on the strength of *independent* convergence:

- Arbon, *How AI Tests Software* — "prefer deterministic oracles; use calibrated semantic or
  visual judges only where needed"
- the finance book ch4 — "if a verdict can be computed by code, it should be, and the LLM
  should only be asked to relay it"

Different authors, different domains, same rule. That is a real convergence.

Then *Testing AI* — **also Arbon** — came into view with chapters directly on oracles, judges
and disagreement. It is tempting to count that as a third arrival. **It isn't.** Reaching for
it would have quietly turned one opinion cited twice into what looked like two confirmations.

## What survives

Q5's legs are: **Arbon (either book, counts once)** + **the finance book ch4/ch11** + **our
own 288 runs.** The second Arbon book is used for *depth on oracle design*, not corroboration.

## Why this failure mode is hard to see

Two books on a shelf look like two sources. Different titles, different covers, different
subjects, different years. Nothing on the artifact tells you the claims are correlated. The
check has to be deliberate: **before counting a convergence, look at the author line.**

Generalises beyond books: the same lab's papers, the same vendor's blog posts, the same
person's talk and repo. Also applies to a source and its own derivative — a book and its
companion skill repo are one source, not two.
